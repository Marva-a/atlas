@pbq firewall-rules
title: Decide firewall actions
kind: grid
obj: 4.1, 3.2
min: 15
lesson: w10-1
intro: Policy: the public may reach the web server (10.0.1.10) only over HTTPS. Administrators on 10.0.9.0/24 may use SSH to the web server. The web server may reach the database (10.0.2.20) on TCP 3306. Nothing else is allowed. Decide what the firewall should do with each connection.
why: Allow only what the policy states and deny the rest. Good rule sets are short, specific and end with a deny.
cols: Source | Destination | Service
choices: Allow | Deny
| 203.0.113.44 | 10.0.1.10 | TCP 443 | Allow | The public may reach the site over HTTPS.
| 203.0.113.44 | 10.0.1.10 | TCP 22 | Deny | SSH is limited to the admin subnet.
| 10.0.9.15 | 10.0.1.10 | TCP 22 | Allow | This host is in the admin subnet.
| 10.0.1.10 | 10.0.2.20 | TCP 3306 | Allow | The application needs its database.
| 203.0.113.44 | 10.0.2.20 | TCP 3306 | Deny | The database must never face the internet.
| 10.0.1.10 | 203.0.113.99 | TCP 25 | Deny | The policy allows no outbound mail from this server.
| 10.0.5.30 | 10.0.1.10 | TCP 3389 | Deny | RDP is not permitted by the policy.

@pbq log-suspicious
title: Find the malicious activity in a log
kind: pick
obj: 2.5, 4.8
min: 15
lesson: w12-4
intro: Select every line that indicates malicious or suspicious activity. Assume the company operates in Canada, with staff on 10.0.0.0/8.
why: Normal logs are mostly routine. Patterns such as one source across many accounts, successful sign-ins following failures, path traversal and sudden large transfers deserve attention.
| 09:01 LOGIN OK user=amy src=10.0.4.15 | no | A normal internal sign-in.
| 09:14 LOGIN FAIL user=bob src=198.51.100.7 | yes | The first of a spraying pattern from one external source.
| 09:14 LOGIN FAIL user=eve src=198.51.100.7 | yes | The same source trying another account.
| 09:15 LOGIN FAIL user=raj src=198.51.100.7 | yes | Continuing across accounts.
| 09:15 LOGIN OK user=raj src=198.51.100.7 | yes | A success from the same source suggests a compromise.
| 09:20 GET /reports/q3.pdf user=amy 200 | no | A routine file request.
| 09:31 GET /download?file=../../etc/passwd 403 | yes | A directory traversal attempt, even though it was blocked.
| 09:40 BACKUP job completed 12GB src=10.0.8.2 | no | A scheduled internal backup.
| 10:02 OUTBOUND user=raj dst=203.0.113.200 bytes=4800000000 | yes | A large transfer soon after a suspicious sign-in.

@pbq access-matrix
title: Assign least-privilege access
kind: grid
obj: 4.5
min: 12
lesson: w11-4
intro: Assign the minimum access that lets each person do the described job.
why: Least privilege means granting only what the task requires. Administrative access is reserved for those who administer the system.
cols: Person | Task | Resource
choices: None | Read | Read and write | Administer
| Payroll clerk | Enter monthly salaries | Payroll system | Read and write | Salary entry requires changes.
| External auditor | Review this year's payroll | Payroll system | Read | Auditors need to see, not to change.
| Marketing intern | Create campaign pages | Payroll system | None | The role has no need for payroll.
| Database administrator | Maintain the payroll database | Payroll system | Administer | This is the admin function of the role.
| Help desk agent | Reset staff passwords | Payroll system | None | A password reset does not need payroll access.
| Payroll manager | Review reports | Payroll reports | Read | Viewing reports requires only reading.

why: Exposure and active exploitation can outweigh raw severity. A low-severity finding on an isolated system can wait.
| CVSS 8.1, internet-facing payment portal, listed in CISA KEV | Exposed, valuable and actively exploited, so it comes first.

| CVSS 9.0, disconnected lab machine awaiting disposal | Isolated, and soon to be retired.

@pbq ir-sequence
title: Respond to a ransomware incident
kind: order
obj: 4.7
min: 10
lesson: w12-2
intro: Put these actions in the order you would perform them.
why: Confirm and contain first, preserve evidence before destroying anything, restore from clean sources, and finish by learning from the incident.
| Validate the alert and declare an incident | Nothing else begins until the incident is confirmed.
| Isolate affected hosts from the network | Stops the spread.
| Preserve memory and disk evidence | Must occur before systems are rebuilt.
| Identify the root cause and scope | Needed for complete eradication.
| Remove the malware and close the entry point | Eradication.
| Restore from clean, verified backups | Recovery must follow eradication.
| Hold a lessons learned review and write the report | Post-incident activity.

obj: 3.2
min: 10
lesson: w8-3
intro: Choose the most appropriate zone for each asset.
why: Grouping by trust and exposure limits what an attacker can reach from any single compromise.
cols: Asset
choices: Screened subnet | Internal server zone | User zone | OT zone | Guest network
| Public-facing web server | Screened subnet | It must be reachable from the internet, but separated from internal systems.
| Customer database | Internal server zone | It should only be reached by the application tier.
| Staff laptops | User zone | Users are separated from servers.
| Factory floor controllers | OT zone | Control systems need strict isolation.
| Visitor phones | Guest network | Unmanaged devices should be kept separate.
| Public-facing mail relay | Screened subnet | It accepts internet connections and forwards inward.

@pbq insecure-config
title: Spot the insecure settings
kind: pick
obj: 4.1, 2.4
min: 10
lesson: w10-2
intro: Select each setting that should be changed.
why: Insecure settings tend to be clear-text protocols, default credentials, excessive permissions or unnecessary exposure.
| SSH: PermitRootLogin yes | yes | Direct root login should be disabled.
| SSH: PasswordAuthentication no | no | Key-based authentication is a stronger choice.
| Telnet service: enabled | yes | Telnet sends credentials in clear text.
| TLS minimum version: 1.2 | no | TLS 1.2 or later is acceptable.
| Admin password: admin | yes | Default or trivial credentials must be changed.
| File permissions on /etc/app.conf: 777 | yes | Everyone can read and change this file.
| Automatic security updates: on | no | This is good practice, subject to change management.
| Database listens on 0.0.0.0 with internet access | yes | Databases should not be exposed to all networks.

@pbq crypto-choice
title: Choose the right cryptography
kind: grid
obj: 1.3
min: 10
lesson: w5-4
intro: Choose the most appropriate technique for each requirement.
why: Encryption is reversible and protects confidentiality, hashing is one-way, and signatures prove origin and integrity.
cols: Requirement
choices: Symmetric encryption | Asymmetric key exchange | Salted password hash | SHA-256 hash | Digital signature | Tokenization
| Encrypt a 50 GB backup quickly | Symmetric encryption | Symmetric encryption is fast for bulk data.
| Agree on a session key across the internet | Asymmetric key exchange | Key exchange solves key sharing.
| Store user passwords | Salted password hash | Passwords should not be reversible.
| Confirm a download is unchanged | SHA-256 hash | Compare digests.
| Prove who approved a document | Digital signature | Provides non-repudiation.
| Keep card numbers out of the application database | Tokenization | Tokens replace the sensitive values.

@pbq risk-math
title: Calculate and treat risk
kind: grid
obj: 5.2
min: 10
lesson: w13-2
intro: Choose the correct answer for each row. Asset value is 200,000 dollars, exposure factor is 25%, and ARO is 0.4.
why: SLE equals asset value multiplied by exposure factor. ALE equals SLE multiplied by ARO.
cols: Question
choices: 50,000 | 20,000 | 80,000 | Mitigating | Transferring | Avoiding | Accepting
| What is the SLE? | 50,000 | 200,000 times 0.25.
| What is the ALE? | 20,000 | 50,000 times 0.4.
| Buying insurance is which treatment? | Transferring | Financial impact moves to the insurer.
| Adding MFA is which treatment? | Mitigating | It reduces likelihood.
| Stopping the risky service is which treatment? | Avoiding | The activity ends.
| Documenting and living with the risk is which treatment? | Accepting | The risk is knowingly kept.

@pbq recovery-plan
title: Choose recovery strategies
kind: grid
obj: 3.4
min: 10
lesson: w9-4
intro: Choose the best answer for each situation.
why: Match the recovery approach to the time and data loss the business can tolerate.
cols: Situation
choices: Hot site | Warm site | Cold site | RTO | RPO | Immutable backups | UPS
| A hospital needs systems back within minutes | Hot site | It is fully ready.
| Equipment can be in place, with restoration in a day or two | Warm site | It has hardware but needs current data.
| A low-priority system can wait for weeks | Cold site | Space and utilities only.
| The maximum acceptable time a service can be down | RTO | Recovery time objective.
| The maximum acceptable data loss, measured in time | RPO | Recovery point objective.
| Ransomware must not be able to change backups | Immutable backups | Copies cannot be altered during the lock period.
| A bridge for a brief power interruption | UPS | Battery power covers short outages.
