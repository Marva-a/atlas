@d w8-2 direct
sector: B2B SaaS
product: In multi-tenant SaaS, many customers share one system, and the product's most important promise is that one customer can never reach another's data. Tenant switching, global search, shared caches and support tools are the places where that promise is most likely to break. Data residency turns an architecture decision into a setting and a sales conversation.
ask eng: At which layer are tenants isolated: application, database or infrastructure?
ask eng: Which features query across tenants, including internal tools?
ask sec: How is tenant isolation tested, and how often?
ask prod: Which customers require a specific data region, and is that a contract term?
ex: Map tenant isolation and data residency | 60
scenario: A collaboration product serves companies in Canada, the EU and the US. A large customer asks where its data is stored and what stops another tenant seeing it. Users can also belong to more than one company's workspace.
problem: The product has no clear model of tenant boundaries for users who belong to several tenants, and nothing tells administrators where their data lives.
task: Draw a data flow diagram for a document, from upload to search index, backup and third-party processors.
task: Mark where tenant boundaries and region boundaries are enforced.
task: Design the workspace switcher so a user always knows which tenant they are acting in.
task: Design the data residency panel, including what cannot be moved.
constraint: Cross-tenant data must never appear in search, autocomplete, notifications or caches.
constraint: Region promises must include backups and third-party processors.
constraint: Moving a tenant between regions may be slow, partial or impossible.
need: Multi-tenant users need to avoid posting to the wrong company.
need: Customer administrators need accurate facts for their own compliance.
need: Sales needs claims that engineering can stand behind.
deliver: A data flow diagram with boundaries marked, plus workspace switcher and residency panel designs.
crit constraints: The diagram shows tenant and region boundaries, including backups and processors.
crit risk: Wrong-tenant actions and cross-tenant leaks through search or notifications are addressed.
crit quality: The active tenant is obvious at the moment a user takes an action.
crit senior: Claims in the interface are checked against what engineering can actually guarantee.
senior: Do not write a data promise into an interface until engineering and legal have confirmed it.
senior: Note where isolation is a business decision too, because stronger isolation costs more and may be sold as a tier.
cq h | Multi-tenancy | Global search in a multi-tenant app shows matching document titles from every tenant, with content hidden until opened. What should a designer flag?
+ Titles are data, so limit results to the current tenant :: Metadata can be sensitive, and it crosses the most important boundary here.
- Results should be grouped alphabetically by tenant name :: This improves the layout while leaving the leak in place.
- Search should display fewer results on each page :: Smaller pages still reveal other tenants' titles.
- Titles are safe because the document body stays hidden :: Titles can themselves reveal deals, patients or plans.

@d w8-4 direct
sector: Identity and access products
product: Security controls fail, and when they do a product must either fail open and keep working unsafely, or fail closed and stop people working. That decision can appear as a login outage, a blocked payment or a safety-critical record that clinicians cannot open.
ask eng: What happens to signed-in sessions when the identity service is unavailable?
ask eng: Can we tell the difference between a denial and a failed policy check?
ask sec: Which functions should fail closed, and which may degrade?
ask prod: What are our customers' obligations during an outage, such as patient care?
ex: Design what happens when the security system fails | 45
scenario: A hospital's scheduling and records application relies on a third-party identity and policy service. During a recent outage, nobody could sign in and staff could not view patient schedules.
problem: The product has no designed behaviour for a failed identity or policy service, so it presents a generic error at the worst possible moment.
task: For each key function, decide whether it should fail open, fail closed or degrade, and explain why.
task: Design the degraded mode and how a user knows they are in it.
task: Design the break-glass route for emergency access, including after-the-fact review.
constraint: Emergency access must be logged, time-limited and reviewed.
constraint: Unknown states must never appear as granted permissions.
constraint: Data entered in degraded mode must be reconciled once service returns.
need: Clinicians need critical information during an outage.
need: Privacy officers need a record of any emergency access.
need: Administrators need to know what is happening and for how long.
deliver: A fail-open, fail-closed or degrade decision table, plus degraded mode and break-glass designs.
crit constraints: Break-glass access is limited in time, logged and reviewed.
crit risk: The decisions consider safety and privacy consequences for each function.
crit quality: Users can tell they are in a degraded state and what is limited.
crit senior: The decision table identifies who must approve each fail-open choice.
senior: Fail-open decisions involve safety and legal judgment, so they need owners beyond the design team.
senior: Rehearsing outages reveals assumptions, so ask to join a recovery exercise.
cq m | Failure modes | A payment approval service is unreachable. Which behaviour is the best default for a large transfer?
+ Hold the transfer and tell the user what will happen :: Failing closed protects integrity while keeping the person informed.
- Approve it automatically and verify it later :: Failing open on a high-value action invites irreversible loss.
- Silently retry forever with no message to the user :: The user is left guessing, and may submit the transfer twice.
- Cancel it and delete all the details the user entered :: This fails safely, but it needlessly destroys the user's work.

@d w9-1 direct
sector: Healthcare applications
product: Data classification decides what a person sees. Masking shows a card's last four digits, tokenization lets staff work with a stand-in, and reveal-on-demand controls record who viewed a value. The three data states also appear in product questions: is it safe at rest, in transit and on screen while in use?
ask eng: Is this field encrypted, hashed, tokenized or masked, and where does that happen?
ask eng: Can a masked value be recovered by an API call that bypasses the interface?
ask sec: How is this data classified, and which rules follow from that?
ask prod: Who truly needs to see the full value, and how often?
ex: Design sensitive data display and access | 60
scenario: Staff at a health insurer use an internal portal showing member records, including government ID numbers, diagnoses and bank details. Today, every staff member sees everything.
problem: Access is all-or-nothing, and there is no record of who viewed which sensitive field.
task: Classify each field on the record and justify each level.
task: Choose masking, tokenization or restricted reveal for each field.
task: Design reveal-with-reason, including the record it creates.
task: Design what appears in exports, printouts, logs and support screenshots.
constraint: Masking must be applied by the server, so the full value does not reach an unauthorized browser.
constraint: Hashing can't be reversed, so a value that must be displayed can't be stored only as a hash.
constraint: Viewing data on screen is the "in use" state, and encryption at rest doesn't protect it.
need: Claims staff need full values occasionally, for a defined reason.
need: Members expect their data to be seen only by those who need it.
need: Privacy officers need to audit unusual access.
deliver: A field classification table, a protection method per field and designs for the reveal flow.
crit constraints: Each protection method is technically appropriate for how that field is used.
crit risk: Exports, logs and screenshots are treated as leak paths.
crit quality: Routine tasks can be finished without revealing unneeded data.
crit senior: Reveal friction is matched to data classification, and it is not the same everywhere.
senior: Masking in the interface alone is decoration, so confirm where the protection is really applied.
senior: Classification belongs to the data owner, so use the organization's scheme and do not invent your own.
cq h | Data protection choices | Staff must verify a member's ID by comparing its last four digits and never need the full value. Which approach fits?
+ Server-side masking that returns only the last four digits :: The full value never reaches the browser, and the task still works.
- Client-side masking with the full value sent to the page :: The full value is exposed to anyone who inspects the response.
- Hashing the value, then displaying the entire hash to staff :: A hash can't be compared by eye with what a member says.
- Encrypting at rest and showing the full value to all staff :: Encryption at rest does not limit who can view it on screen.

@d w9-2 direct
sector: B2B SaaS

ex: Design data export and deletion | 60
scenario: A B2B product with customers in the EU receives requests from individual users to export and delete their data. The customer organization is the data controller, and it also has retention rules.
problem: Requests are handled by email and spreadsheets, with no verification, unclear ownership and no way to say what was deleted.
task: Map the data roles: controller, processor, owner and custodian for this product.
task: Design the request, verification, approval and progress flow.
task: Design the export package experience, including secure delivery and expiry.
task: Write honest messages for partial deletion, retention holds and backup timing.
constraint: An export is a concentrated copy of sensitive data, so it needs strong verification and expiry.
constraint: A legal hold can override a deletion request.
constraint: The customer organization may need to approve or be told about requests.
need: Individuals need to know what happened to their data, and when.
need: Customer administrators need to meet their own obligations.
need: Legal and privacy staff need a defensible record.
deliver: A flow from request to confirmation, an export delivery design and a set of status messages.
crit constraints: The flow respects controller and processor roles and legal holds.
crit risk: An attacker requesting an export is treated as a real scenario.
crit quality: Status messages are specific about what is done, what is pending and why.
crit senior: The design is reviewed against actual legal requirements through named stakeholders.
senior: You are not giving legal advice, so bring questions to privacy counsel and let them confirm the requirement.
senior: Deletion that can't be verified should not be described as complete.
cq m | Data rights | A user requests deletion, but a legal hold applies to some records. What should the product say?
+ What was deleted, what remains and why it remains :: Honest specifics respect the user and the legal obligation.
- All of your data has now been permanently deleted :: This would be false, and it could create legal exposure.
- Your request failed, so please try again next month :: The request did not fail, and the user learns nothing.
- Nothing, because legal holds are confidential processes :: The hold's details may be limited, but the outcome should still be communicated.

@d w10-3 direct
sector: B2B SaaS
product: Sending email from a customer's own domain requires SPF, DKIM and DMARC records, which is a difficult task to hand to a marketing manager. Products that handle this well turn a technical protocol into a guided, verifiable setup, and they say plainly what is at risk if it stays incomplete.

problem: The current settings page lists DNS records with no explanation, and the person using it is often not the person who controls DNS.

task: Design verification, propagation wait, partial success and failure states.
task: Show the path from a monitoring DMARC policy to enforcement.

constraint: Moving to a reject policy too early can block legitimate mail.
constraint: Records must be copied exactly, so manual retyping should be avoided.

need: DNS administrators need exact values with no marketing context.

crit constraints: The flow represents SPF, DKIM and DMARC correctly and in the right order.
crit risk: The design explains the harm of spoofing and of an early reject policy.
crit quality: A non-technical user can complete their part and hand off the rest.
crit senior: The design defines success in terms of authenticated mail, and not of completed screens.

cq m | Email authentication | Which step should a setup wizard recommend first for a domain with an unknown sending landscape?
+ Start DMARC in monitoring mode and review reports :: This reveals legitimate senders before anything is blocked.
- Publish DMARC with an immediate reject policy today :: Legitimate mail from unknown senders could be blocked.
- Remove all SPF records before adding any DKIM keys :: This removes existing protection and breaks authentication.
- Wait until spoofing has been reported by customers :: Waiting leaves the domain exposed and gives no data.

cq m | Vulnerability prioritization | Which finding usually deserves attention first?
+ A medium finding on an exposed system under active attack :: Real exploitation and exposure raise the actual risk.
- A critical finding on an isolated, decommissioned test machine :: The score is high, but the exposure and impact are low.
- The oldest finding in the list, whatever it happens to affect :: Age alone doesn't measure risk.
- The finding with the longest technical description available :: Description length says nothing about risk.

cq h | Excessive OAuth scopes | An analytics app needs to read project names, yet it asks for full read and write on all workspace data. What should the product support?
