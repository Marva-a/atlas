Managing hardware, software and data assets

Lesson 41 · Week 10 · Domain 4: Security Operations · Objective 4.2 · Beginner · about 120 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Professional relevance for cross-functional work

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Follow an asset through its life cycle.
  • Explain why an accurate inventory matters.
  • Choose the proper disposal method.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

You cannot protect what you do not know you have. Assets that nobody tracks do not get patched, and discarded drives have exposed a surprising amount of sensitive data.

In plain language

Think of a company car. It is planned, bought, assigned to a driver, tracked and serviced, then sold or scrapped. You would clear out the glove compartment before selling it. Hardware, software and data need the same care.

2. Technical deep dive

The asset management life cycle has these stages. Planning/scoping decides what is needed. The acquisition/procurement process covers buying through approved suppliers, with security requirements. Assignment/accounting records who owns or uses an asset and adds it to inventory. Monitoring/asset tracking keeps records current, for example through discovery scans. Disposal/decommissioning retires an asset securely.

NIST SP 800-88 describes three main sanitization outcomes: clear, using logical methods to protect against simple recovery; purge, using stronger techniques that defeat laboratory recovery, such as cryptographic erase; and destroy, making the media unusable. Keep a certificate of destruction when third parties do this work. Data assets need the same tracking and also have owners, classifications and retention periods. Software assets need license and version tracking.

MethodProtects againstExample
ClearSimple recoveryOverwriting with standard tools
PurgeLaboratory recoveryCryptographic erase

Walkthrough: Decommission a server

  1. Confirm its owner approves retirement, and check dependencies.
  2. Migrate or archive data according to retention rules.
  3. Revoke its credentials and certificates and remove its DNS and firewall entries.
  4. Sanitize or destroy the disks in accordance with data sensitivity.
  5. Update the inventory and keep the record.

Hands-on lab

Build a small asset register

About 30 min. Spreadsheet

  1. Record ten assets you own: devices, software and data sets.
  2. For each, add owner, location, classification and end-of-support date.
  3. Mark the disposal method you would use.
  4. Find the end-of-support date for your OS on the vendor's site.

Expected: A register that shows which assets are near or past end of support.

An up-to-date register is the base of patching, incident response and audits.

Done when:

  • Your register has all fields for ten assets.
  • You selected appropriate disposal methods.

3. See it in real products

Asset inventories appear as device lists, app inventories and connected-app pages. A product can only help someone secure what it can show them, so an accurate, searchable inventory with ownership and last-seen dates is itself a security feature.

In security operations

  • A forgotten test server, not on any inventory, runs out-of-date software and becomes the entry point for a breach.
  • An organization resells old laptops without wiping them, and customer data ends up on the second-hand market.

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: A company is retiring encrypted SSDs that held confidential data, and wants to reuse them within the company. What is an appropriate method?

Compare your reasoning

Purge using cryptographic erase, then verify. Destroying them would prevent reuse, and clearing alone may not be enough for confidential data on SSDs.

1. Why is an accurate asset inventory important?

  • You cannot protect or patch what you do not know exists
  • It replaces the need for firewalls
  • It encrypts data automatically
  • It removes the need for backups
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: You cannot protect or patch what you do not know exists. Inventory underpins other controls.
  • Incorrect: It replaces the need for firewalls. It does not.
  • Incorrect: It encrypts data automatically. It does not.
  • Incorrect: It removes the need for backups. It does not.

2. Put the stages of the asset life cycle in order.

Correct order
  1. Planning/scoping
  2. Acquisition/procurement
  3. Assignment/accounting
  4. Monitoring/asset tracking
  5. Disposal/decommissioning

Each stage relies on records from the one before.

3. Which sanitization result protects against laboratory recovery while still allowing reuse?

  • Purge
  • Clear
  • Destroy
  • Deleting the files
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Purge. Purge defeats laboratory techniques, and the media can be reused.
  • Incorrect: Clear. Clear protects only against simple recovery.
  • Incorrect: Destroy. The media cannot be reused after this.
  • Incorrect: Deleting the files. Deletion does not sanitize.

4. A server is retired, but its service account stays active. What risk results?

  • A stale credential remains that could be abused
  • The server's disks become faster
  • CVSS scores increase
  • The DMARC policy breaks
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: A stale credential remains that could be abused. Decommissioning must also remove access.
  • Incorrect: The server's disks become faster. This is unrelated.
  • Incorrect: CVSS scores increase. This is unrelated.
  • Incorrect: The DMARC policy breaks. This is unrelated.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Assuming that deleting files or formatting a drive destroys the data.
  • Tracking hardware but forgetting data and software.

Summary

  • Assets need an owner at every stage.
  • Inventory drives other security activity.
  • Dispose of media according to data sensitivity.
