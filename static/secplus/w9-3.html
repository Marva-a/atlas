Resilience: sites, platform diversity, redundancy and power

Lesson 36 · Week 9 · Domain 3: Security Architecture · Objective 3.4 · Intermediate · about 120 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Professional relevance for cross-functional work

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Compare hot, warm and cold sites.
  • Choose redundancy strategies.
  • Select the right power protection.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

Availability is one-third of the CIA triad. Ransomware, storms and hardware failure all test whether a business can keep working.

In plain language

A hot site is a furnished, stocked spare office you can walk into right away. A warm site has furniture but needs setup. A cold site is an empty room with power and a lease. Redundancy is keeping a spare tire, and load balancing is sharing the weight among several tires.

2. Technical deep dive

Site considerations: a hot site is fully equipped and current, offering fast recovery at high cost. A warm site has hardware but needs current data and setup. A cold site has space and utilities only. Environmental factors mean a recovery site should not share the same flood plain, power grid or earthquake zone.

Platform diversity reduces common-mode failure through a different vendor platform, different hardware or different virtualization technology.

Redundancy strategies: load balancing spreads requests across servers. Clustering groups servers so that one can take over from another. Autoscaling adds or removes capacity automatically. High availability means designing to minimize downtime. Multicloud systems use multiple providers. Storage redundancy includes RAID and replication. Power protection includes an uninterruptible power supply (UPS) for short-term battery power, a redundant power supply (RPS) in a device, a power generator for long outages and a surge protector for voltage spikes.

A load balancer shares the work and routes around a failed server, so one failure does not take the service down.

Walkthrough: Plan power for a server room

  1. Add surge protection against spikes.
  2. Add a UPS sized to bridge the gap until the generator starts.
  3. Add a generator for long outages, with fuel and testing.
  4. Use dual power supplies connected to separate circuits.
  5. Test it, because untested power protection is only a hope.

3. See it in real products

Resilience appears as status pages, read-only modes and regional failover. Redundancy decisions determine whether a user experiences a moment of slowness or an afternoon offline.

In security operations

  • A data centre rides through a brief outage on UPS while its generators start.
  • An online shop's load balancer stops sending traffic to a failed server, and customers do not notice.

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: A business needs to resume in about two days after a disaster and wants moderate cost. Its equipment can be pre-installed, but data would need restoring. Which site fits?

Compare your reasoning

A warm site. It has equipment in place but needs current data and setup, with cost and recovery time between hot and cold.

1. Which site offers the fastest recovery?

  • Hot
  • Warm
  • Cold
  • Mobile tape vault
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Hot. It is fully equipped and current.
  • Incorrect: Warm. It requires data and setup.
  • Incorrect: Cold. It has only basic facilities.
  • Incorrect: Mobile tape vault. This is storage only.

2. Which device keeps equipment running briefly during an outage?

  • UPS
  • Surge protector
  • Load balancer
  • CASB
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: UPS. Batteries give an immediate bridge.
  • Incorrect: Surge protector. This handles spikes only.
  • Incorrect: Load balancer. This distributes requests.
  • Incorrect: CASB. This is a cloud security tool.

3. What is the main role of a load balancer?

  • Distribute requests across multiple servers
  • Encrypt data at rest
  • Provide battery power
  • Detect phishing
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Distribute requests across multiple servers. This improves availability and capacity.
  • Incorrect: Encrypt data at rest. That is a storage control.
  • Incorrect: Provide battery power. That is a UPS.
  • Incorrect: Detect phishing. That is an email security function.

4. Why should a recovery site not be located close to the main site?

  • A regional event could affect both
  • Data cannot be transmitted across short distances
  • Warm sites must be in another country by law
  • It would reduce the backup's integrity
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: A regional event could affect both. Shared environmental risk defeats the purpose.
  • Incorrect: Data cannot be transmitted across short distances. It can.
  • Incorrect: Warm sites must be in another country by law. There is no such general rule.
  • Incorrect: It would reduce the backup's integrity. Distance does not affect integrity.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Confusing UPS with a generator. A UPS bridges briefly, and a generator sustains longer.
  • Confusing redundancy with backup. Redundancy keeps things running, and backups restore lost data.

Summary

  • Site type trades cost for recovery speed.
  • Redundancy removes single points of failure.
  • Power protection comes in layers.
