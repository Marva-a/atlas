Design brief: Enterprise identity for a multi-tenant SaaS product

About 90 min

Scenario: A 3,000-person customer is moving onto your multi-tenant collaboration product. It requires SSO, automated removal of leavers, custom roles, contractor access and a quarterly access review.

Problem statement: Each capability has been designed in isolation, and nobody has followed one person from joining to leaving the company.

Your design task:

  • Map one contractor's life cycle: invited, provisioned, changing role, reviewed and offboarded.
  • Identify every place the identity, role or tenant boundary could fail along that journey.
  • Design the administrator's single view of a person's access and where it comes from.
  • Write a one-page design decision record covering your largest trade-off.

Technical security constraints:

  • Offboarding must remove access, end sessions and handle what the person owned.
  • Guests must be contained within the tenants and spaces they were invited to.
  • Every privileged change must be attributable in the audit log.

User needs:

  • IT administrators need one accurate answer to "who has access?".
  • Contractors need to get started quickly and with limited access.
  • Auditors need evidence of quarterly reviews.

Expected deliverable: A life cycle map, an access view design and a design decision record.

Evaluation criteria:

  • SSO, roles, tenant boundaries and audit are consistent across the journey.
  • Orphaned access and cross-tenant exposure are addressed.
  • The access view explains the source of each permission.
  • The decision record states alternatives, the trade-off and who needs to agree.

Senior-level considerations:

  • Follow people through time, because many identity failures happen during changes and not at sign-in.
  • Name which parts need security architecture approval.

A design review is not a security sign-off. Have security engineers confirm technical decisions.

Design critique

1. A contractor is disabled in the identity provider but keeps access through a local guest account. What did the design miss?

  • A single life cycle that also covers non-SSO access paths
  • A second confirmation before disabling the contractor
  • A larger contractor badge next to the person's name
  • A shorter password policy for the contractor accounts
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: A single life cycle that also covers non-SSO access paths. Every path to access has to be included in offboarding.
  • Incorrect: A second confirmation before disabling the contractor. Extra confirmation doesn't reach the guest account.
  • Incorrect: A larger contractor badge next to the person's name. Labelling doesn't remove the remaining access.
  • Incorrect: A shorter password policy for the contractor accounts. Password length is unrelated to the leftover access path.
