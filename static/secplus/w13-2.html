Risk management

Lesson 52 · Week 13 · Domain 5: Security Program Management and Oversight · Objective 5.2 · Intermediate · about 100 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Direct product design relevance

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Identify, assess and analyze risk.
  • Calculate SLE and ALE.
  • Choose a treatment and maintain a risk register.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

Security is mostly about deciding which risks matter and what to do about them, with limited money and time.

In plain language

Consider home insurance. You think about what you own, what could happen, how likely it is and what it would cost. Then you lock the doors, buy insurance, move out of the flood zone or simply accept the small risks.

2. Technical deep dive

Risk identification includes asset identification and stakeholder ownership. Risk assessment uses scoring and categorization. Risk analysis considers impact, likelihood or probability, the owner, current mitigations, and whether the method is qualitative (categories like high or low) or quantitative (numbers).

A risk register records risks, owners, scores and treatments, and supports communication and regular reviews. Risk treatment options are transferring (such as insurance), accepting, avoiding (stopping the activity) and mitigating (reducing the risk).

Business-level considerations include a business impact analysis (BIA), which identifies critical functions and the effect of losing them; risk appetite, the amount of risk an organization will pursue; residual risk, which remains after controls; stakeholder involvement; management oversight; and regulatory and legal requirements.

Quantitative risk: single loss expectancy (SLE) is asset value multiplied by exposure factor. The annualized rate of occurrence (ARO) is how many times per year an event is expected. Annualized loss expectancy (ALE) is SLE multiplied by ARO.

Risk is likelihood and impact together. The matrix turns a long list of worries into an order for dealing with them.

Walkthrough: Calculate quantitative risk

  1. Asset value is 100,000 dollars, and exposure factor is 40%.
  2. SLE equals 100,000 times 0.40, which is 40,000.
  3. ARO is 0.5, meaning once every two years.
  4. ALE equals 40,000 times 0.5, which is 20,000 a year.
  5. Compare that figure with what the control would cost.

3. See it in real products

Risk management is how a team decides whether to ship. A feature with a known weakness might be accepted, mitigated or held back, and those decisions need to be recorded by someone with authority. Security requirements written during discovery are far cheaper than findings written after launch.

In security operations

  • A laptop worth 2,000 dollars with an exposure factor of 100% has an SLE of 2,000. If two are lost a year, the ALE is 4,000. A control costing 500 a year that halves losses saves more than it costs.
  • A company buys cyber insurance, which transfers some financial impact but does not remove the risk of reputational harm.

4. Apply it as a Product Designer

Design brief: Write security requirements during discovery

About 45 min

Scenario: Your team is starting discovery on bulk-invite for workspaces: admins upload a CSV to invite thousands of users, and invitations can grant any role.

Problem statement: Security review usually happens just before launch, when changes are expensive and contentious.

Your design task:

  • Identify the assets, actors and likely misuse.
  • Write a security requirements document with testable requirements.
  • Create a short risk register with likelihood, impact, treatment and owner.
  • List questions for engineering, security and product.

Technical security constraints:

  • CSV contents are untrusted and may contain invalid or malicious values.
  • Requirements must be testable, not general wishes such as "be secure".
  • Designers propose treatments, while risk acceptance belongs to the authorized owner.

User needs:

  • Engineers need clear requirements early.
  • Security staff need enough context to review.
  • Product managers need the trade-offs to plan a release.

Expected deliverable: A one-page security requirements document and a risk register with at least five risks.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Requirements are specific, testable and tied to the constraints.
  • Risks include misuse by authorized users as well as outside attackers.
  • The document can be read and used by non-security colleagues.
  • Each risk has a treatment and a named owner, with acceptance left to the authorized person.

Senior-level considerations:

  • Introducing security questions in discovery is one of the most valuable things a designer can do.
  • Your risk register supports the decision, and accountability stays with the risk owner.

A design review is not a security sign-off. Have security engineers confirm technical decisions.

Questions a Senior Product Designer should ask

Engineering:

  • What would it cost to reduce this risk now, compared with later?

Security:

  • How do we score risk here, and who can accept it?

Product:

  • What is the cost of delaying this release?

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: A server costing 50,000 dollars would lose 20% of its value in a flood, and floods are expected once every ten years. What is the ALE?

Compare your reasoning

SLE is 50,000 times 0.2, which is 10,000. ARO is 0.1. ALE is 10,000 times 0.1, which is 1,000 dollars a year.

1. Buying cyber insurance is an example of which treatment?

  • Transferring
  • Avoiding
  • Accepting
  • Mitigating
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Transferring. Financial impact shifts to the insurer.
  • Incorrect: Avoiding. The activity continues.
  • Incorrect: Accepting. Action was taken.
  • Incorrect: Mitigating. The likelihood of an attack is unchanged.

2. SLE is 8,000 dollars and ARO is 3. What is the ALE?

  • 24,000 dollars
  • 2,667 dollars
  • 8,003 dollars
  • 8,000 dollars
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: 24,000 dollars. ALE equals SLE multiplied by ARO.
  • Incorrect: 2,667 dollars. This divides instead of multiplying.
  • Incorrect: 8,003 dollars. This adds them.
  • Incorrect: 8,000 dollars. This ignores ARO.

3. What is residual risk?

  • The risk that remains after controls are applied
  • The risk before any controls exist
  • A risk that has been transferred
  • Money saved by controls
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: The risk that remains after controls are applied. No control removes all risk.
  • Incorrect: The risk before any controls exist. That is inherent risk.
  • Incorrect: A risk that has been transferred. Transfer is a treatment.
  • Incorrect: Money saved by controls. This is not risk.

4. A company stops offering a feature because its risk cannot be justified. Which treatment is this?

  • Avoiding
  • Transferring
  • Accepting
  • Mitigating
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Avoiding. Stopping the activity removes the risk.
  • Incorrect: Transferring. No other party took it on.
  • Incorrect: Accepting. The company did not keep the risk.
  • Incorrect: Mitigating. The feature was removed, rather than made safer.

5. Match each term with its meaning.

Correct matches
  • SLE: Cost of a single event
  • ARO: Expected occurrences per year
  • ALE: Expected annual cost
  • Risk appetite: Amount of risk willing to be taken

Memorize the formula: ALE equals SLE times ARO.

B. Product design assessment

Rate your work in the interactive app, where it counts toward design competency only.

1. Which is the best security requirement for bulk invite?

  • Invited roles can't exceed the role of the inviting admin
  • The bulk invitation feature must be secure and trustworthy
  • The team should think carefully about security for invitations
  • Invitations should follow general industry best practices always
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Invited roles can't exceed the role of the inviting admin. It is specific, testable and limits privilege escalation.
  • Incorrect: The bulk invitation feature must be secure and trustworthy. This can't be tested or built against.
  • Incorrect: The team should think carefully about security for invitations. This is an intention and doesn't describe any behaviour.
  • Incorrect: Invitations should follow general industry best practices always. It is too vague to verify.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Thinking transferring removes the risk altogether. Reputational harm stays.
  • Accepting a risk without documenting who accepted it.

Summary

  • Identify, assess, analyze, treat and review.
  • SLE times ARO equals ALE.
  • Every risk needs an owner.
