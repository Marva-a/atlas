Security awareness

Lesson 56 · Week 13 · Domain 5: Security Program Management and Oversight · Objective 5.6 · Beginner · about 100 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Professional relevance for cross-functional work

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Choose training types and delivery mechanisms.
  • Measure whether training works.
  • Build a topic plan including BEC and operational security.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

People are both a common target and an early warning system. Good awareness programs change behaviour, and that is what should be measured.

In plain language

Awareness training is like fire drills. A single talk is forgotten. Regular, specific practice, followed by feedback, means that people know what to do when it matters.

2. Technical deep dive

Types of training include initial/onboarding when someone joins, ongoing, targeted (for a role or risk) and corrective (following a mistake). Delivery mechanisms include a learning management system (LMS), self-service portals, one-to-one and one-to-many sessions.

Reporting and monitoring of effectiveness uses metrics (such as phishing report rates), managerial reports and personnel behavior risk scoring, which should be used carefully and fairly. Common training topics include social engineering, emerging security topics, password and credential management, remote work/teleworking/hybrid, BYOD, business email compromise (BEC), removable media and cables, situational awareness and operational security.

Walkthrough: Build an awareness plan

  1. Identify the top human risks.
  2. Decide on training type and delivery for each.
  3. Set measures, such as time to report phishing.
  4. Run the training and communicate results to managers.
  5. Adjust it according to results.

Hands-on lab

Design a phishing awareness program

About 30 min. Worksheet

  1. Draft a one-year plan: four topics, audience and delivery for each.
  2. Define two metrics and targets.
  3. Draft a short message that tells staff how to report a suspicious email.
  4. Note how you would keep scoring fair and proportionate.

Expected: A plan with audiences, delivery, metrics and a report message.

The aim is changed behaviour, and a culture in which reporting is encouraged, not embarrassment.

Done when:

  • Your plan includes four topics and two metrics.
  • Your message tells staff exactly what to do.

3. See it in real products

Security awareness is partly a product problem. Clear warnings, easy phishing-report buttons and well-timed advice work better than annual training alone. Punishing people who click can discourage the reporting that the organization most needs.

In security operations

  • After a phishing simulation, those who clicked get short corrective training, and those who report are thanked. Reporting rate is tracked, more than click rate alone.
  • A finance team receives targeted training on BEC, including a call-back step for any payment change.

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: The finance team was caught by a fake invoice email from a supplier's compromised account. Which training is most appropriate?

Compare your reasoning

Targeted training on business email compromise, including verifying payment changes through a known phone number.

1. Training given after an employee falls for a phishing test is which type?

  • Corrective
  • Initial
  • Onboarding
  • Benchmarking
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Corrective. It follows a specific mistake.
  • Incorrect: Initial. This occurs on joining.
  • Incorrect: Onboarding. This occurs on joining.
  • Incorrect: Benchmarking. This compares with peers.

2. Which metric best shows staff are becoming a stronger detection layer?

  • The rate at which staff report suspected phishing
  • The number of slides in the training
  • The size of the training video files
  • The number of firewall rules
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: The rate at which staff report suspected phishing. It measures positive behaviour.
  • Incorrect: The number of slides in the training. This measures content, not change.
  • Incorrect: The size of the training video files. This is irrelevant.
  • Incorrect: The number of firewall rules. This is unrelated.

3. An email appears to come from a supplier, asking to change bank details. What should staff do?

  • Verify by calling a known number from existing records
  • Reply to the email to confirm
  • Make the payment if the logo looks correct
  • Forward it to all staff
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Verify by calling a known number from existing records. Using an independent channel defeats the fraud.
  • Incorrect: Reply to the email to confirm. The attacker may control the mailbox.
  • Incorrect: Make the payment if the logo looks correct. Logos can be copied.
  • Incorrect: Forward it to all staff. This doesn't verify anything.

4. Match each to its delivery mechanism.

Correct matches
  • An online course that records completion: LMS
  • A personal coaching session: One-to-one
  • An all-staff briefing: One-to-many
  • A help site staff can browse: Self-service portals

Delivery should fit the audience and message.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Measuring only completion, not behaviour.
  • Using punishment in a way that discourages reporting.

Summary

  • Match training type to need.
  • Measure behaviour.
  • Teach people what to do and how to report it.
