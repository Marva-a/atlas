Design brief: Healthcare account takeover, from alert to post-incident review

About 90 min

Scenario: A monitoring alert shows that a patient portal's staff account has viewed hundreds of records overnight. The staff member says they were asleep.

Problem statement: The product has separate tools for alerting, account action, audit and communications, which makes a coordinated response slow and error-prone.

Your design task:

  • Build a timeline of what the investigator needs at each stage of incident response.
  • Design the investigation and containment view.
  • Design evidence handling that doesn't undermine the investigation.
  • Draft patient communication states, leaving final wording to legal and privacy staff.
  • Write a post-incident review with product changes and their owners.

Technical security constraints:

  • Containment actions must not destroy evidence.
  • Health privacy obligations may set notification rules, confirmed by privacy counsel.
  • Investigators can see sensitive records, so their access must also be logged.

User needs:

  • Investigators need one timeline with reliable data.
  • Clinical staff need disruption kept to a minimum.
  • Patients need truthful, timely and clear information.

Expected deliverable: An incident journey, investigation view and post-incident review with product actions.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Stages follow the incident response process, and evidence is preserved.
  • The design considers ongoing attacker access and insider possibilities.
  • The investigator's next action is clear under pressure.
  • The review turns findings into funded product changes with owners.

Senior-level considerations:

  • Do not presume that the staff member is innocent or guilty, and design the process accordingly.
  • Communications are a joint decision with legal, privacy and clinical leaders.

A design review is not a security sign-off. Have security engineers confirm technical decisions.

Design critique

1. An investigator wants to wipe the suspect's workstation immediately to stop the attack. What should the process support first?

  • Isolating it and preserving evidence before any wipe
  • Wiping it straight away, since speed matters most of all
  • Waiting until the next quarterly review before acting at all
  • Sending the entire company the staff member's name
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Isolating it and preserving evidence before any wipe. Isolation contains the threat, and evidence stays available.
  • Incorrect: Wiping it straight away, since speed matters most of all. This can destroy the evidence needed to understand the incident.
  • Incorrect: Waiting until the next quarterly review before acting at all. Delay allows the attack to continue.
  • Incorrect: Sending the entire company the staff member's name. This is unfair, premature and harmful to the investigation.
