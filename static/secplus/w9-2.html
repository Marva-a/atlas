Data roles, handling, life cycle and compliance

Lesson 35 · Week 9 · Domain 3: Security Architecture · Objective 3.3 · Intermediate · about 120 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Direct product design relevance

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Assign data protection roles.
  • Apply marking, geofencing and placement rules.
  • Follow data through its life cycle and link it to compliance needs.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

When everyone is responsible, no one is. Clearly assigned data roles and known life-cycle stages are the backbone of privacy obligations.

In plain language

A library has an owner who sets the rules, a librarian who looks after the books and an assistant who checks them out. Data has similar roles. Data also has a life, from being created until it is securely destroyed.

2. Technical deep dive

Data protection roles: the data owner is accountable for the data and decides how it is classified. The data custodian implements and maintains the technical controls. The data steward looks after data quality and proper use. The data operator handles day-to-day processing. The data controller decides why and how personal data is processed. A data subprocessor is engaged by a processor to carry out part of the processing.

Data handling covers: endpoints, where data is often copied and lost; marking and labeling, which show classification; geofencing, which restricts access by location; data location, where data physically resides; and data placement, deciding where data is stored according to its sensitivity.

The data management life cycle covers creation, management, distribution, retention and disposal. Data compliance is shaped by standards and by the kind of data: health data, personal information, financial data, child/minor data, intellectual property and legal data. Examples include HIPAA for US health data, GDPR in the EU and PIPEDA in Canada, and PCI DSS for payment card data.

Walkthrough: Follow a customer record through the life cycle

  1. Creation: a customer signs up, and the record is classified.
  2. Management: staff update it under access controls.
  3. Distribution: a shipping partner receives only the address.
  4. Retention: it is kept for the legally required period.
  5. Disposal: it is securely deleted at the end of the period.

3. See it in real products

Data subject rights become product flows: download my data, delete my account, correct this record. Deletion is especially tricky because data lives in backups, logs and third-party tools. Retention rules or legal holds can also require the opposite of what the user asks.

In security operations

  • A hospital (controller) uses a billing company (processor), which uses a cloud host as a subprocessor. Contracts must cover each of these relationships.
  • A company allows access to a finance app only from Canada, using geofencing.

4. Apply it as a Product Designer

Design brief: Design data export and deletion

About 60 min

Scenario: A B2B product with customers in the EU receives requests from individual users to export and delete their data. The customer organization is the data controller, and it also has retention rules.

Problem statement: Requests are handled by email and spreadsheets, with no verification, unclear ownership and no way to say what was deleted.

Your design task:

  • Map the data roles: controller, processor, owner and custodian for this product.
  • Design the request, verification, approval and progress flow.
  • Design the export package experience, including secure delivery and expiry.
  • Write honest messages for partial deletion, retention holds and backup timing.

Technical security constraints:

  • An export is a concentrated copy of sensitive data, so it needs strong verification and expiry.
  • A legal hold can override a deletion request.
  • The customer organization may need to approve or be told about requests.

User needs:

  • Individuals need to know what happened to their data, and when.
  • Customer administrators need to meet their own obligations.
  • Legal and privacy staff need a defensible record.

Expected deliverable: A flow from request to confirmation, an export delivery design and a set of status messages.

Evaluation criteria:

  • The flow respects controller and processor roles and legal holds.
  • An attacker requesting an export is treated as a real scenario.
  • Status messages are specific about what is done, what is pending and why.
  • The design is reviewed against actual legal requirements through named stakeholders.

Senior-level considerations:

  • You are not giving legal advice, so bring questions to privacy counsel and let them confirm the requirement.
  • Deletion that can't be verified should not be described as complete.

A design review is not a security sign-off. Have security engineers confirm technical decisions.

Questions a Senior Product Designer should ask

Engineering:

  • Where does this person's data live, including backups and third parties?
  • How long until deletion is actually complete everywhere?

Security:

  • How do we verify the identity of the person requesting an export?

Product:

  • What are our legal retention obligations, and who confirms them?

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: A database administrator applies encryption and access controls according to rules set by a business leader, who is accountable for the data. Which roles are these?

Compare your reasoning

The database administrator is the data custodian, and the business leader is the data owner.

1. Who is accountable for classifying a data set?

  • The data owner
  • The data custodian
  • The data subprocessor
  • An external auditor
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: The data owner. Accountability sits with the owner.
  • Incorrect: The data custodian. The custodian implements controls.
  • Incorrect: The data subprocessor. This is a third party.
  • Incorrect: An external auditor. Auditors assess, and do not own.

2. A retailer decides what customer data to collect and why, and a payroll vendor processes it as directed. What is the retailer?

  • The data controller
  • The data custodian
  • The subprocessor
  • The data steward
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: The data controller. It determines the purpose and means.
  • Incorrect: The data custodian. That role implements controls.
  • Incorrect: The subprocessor. That is a further third party.
  • Incorrect: The data steward. That focuses on data quality.

3. Put the stages of the data life cycle in order.

Correct order
  1. Creation
  2. Management
  3. Distribution
  4. Retention
  5. Disposal

Data must be created and managed before it is distributed, and retained before it is disposed of.

4. A company blocks sign-ins from outside its operating region. What technique is this?

  • Geofencing
  • Masking
  • Tokenization
  • Hashing
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Geofencing. Access depends on location.
  • Incorrect: Masking. Masking hides values.
  • Incorrect: Tokenization. This substitutes values.
  • Incorrect: Hashing. This creates digests.

B. Product design assessment

Rate your work in the interactive app, where it counts toward design competency only.

1. A user requests deletion, but a legal hold applies to some records. What should the product say?

  • What was deleted, what remains and why it remains
  • All of your data has now been permanently deleted
  • Your request failed, so please try again next month
  • Nothing, because legal holds are confidential processes
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: What was deleted, what remains and why it remains. Honest specifics respect the user and the legal obligation.
  • Incorrect: All of your data has now been permanently deleted. This would be false, and it could create legal exposure.
  • Incorrect: Your request failed, so please try again next month. The request did not fail, and the user learns nothing.
  • Incorrect: Nothing, because legal holds are confidential processes. The hold's details may be limited, but the outcome should still be communicated.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Confusing owner and custodian. The owner is accountable, while the custodian operates the controls.
  • Keeping data indefinitely, since unneeded data is liability.

Summary

  • Data roles make accountability clear.
  • Handling rules apply on endpoints and in storage.
  • Data has a life cycle, ending with secure disposal.
