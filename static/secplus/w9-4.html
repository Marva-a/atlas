Backups, testing, disaster recovery and recovery metrics

Lesson 37 · Week 9 · Domain 3: Security Architecture · Objective 3.4 · Intermediate · about 120 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Professional relevance for cross-functional work

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Design a backup approach that resists ransomware.
  • Define and calculate RTO, RPO, MTTR and MTBF.
  • Distinguish disaster recovery, business continuity and testing types.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

A backup that has never been restored is an assumption. Recovery metrics turn vague hopes into commitments that a business can plan and pay for.

In plain language

RPO is how far back you can afford to go: if the last save was four hours ago, you lose four hours of work. RTO is how long you can afford to be down. MTBF is how long something tends to work before breaking, and MTTR is how long it takes to fix.

2. Technical deep dive

Backups involve retention (how long copies are kept), immutability (copies cannot be altered or deleted for a period, which resists ransomware), scope (what is included, such as full, incremental or differential) and restoration testing (proving a backup can be recovered). A common guideline is three copies, on two media, with one offsite.

Testing types: failover moves work to a standby system. Simulation walks through a scenario without affecting production. Parallel processing runs recovery systems alongside production to compare results. Disaster recovery focuses on restoring technology after a major event. Business continuity keeps the critical functions of the organization running. Capacity planning ensures that people, technology and infrastructure can meet expected demand.

Recovery metrics: the recovery time objective (RTO) is the maximum acceptable time to restore a service. The recovery point objective (RPO) is the maximum acceptable data loss measured in time. Mean time to repair (MTTR) is the average time to fix a failure. Mean time between failures (MTBF) is the average operating time between failures. MTBF equals total operating time divided by the number of failures.

RPO looks backward from the failure to the last backup. RTO looks forward from the failure to recovery.

Walkthrough: Work out recovery metrics

  1. A database is backed up every 6 hours, so the RPO cannot be better than 6 hours.
  2. A required restore takes 3 hours, plus 1 hour of checks, so RTO must be at least 4 hours.
  3. A server ran 2,000 hours and failed 5 times, so MTBF is 400 hours.
  4. Those 5 repairs took 10 hours in total, so MTTR is 2 hours.

Hands-on lab

Back up and actually restore

About 35 min. tar in your lab VM

  1. Create a folder with five text files in your VM.
  2. Run tar -czf backup.tar.gz myfolder and record the time.
  3. Delete one file, and change another.
  4. Restore into a new folder with mkdir restore, then tar -xzf backup.tar.gz -C restore, and confirm the original contents using diff.
  5. State the RPO your backup represents.

Expected: The restored copy matches the state at the time of backup, and later edits are lost.

The difference between the backup time and the failure time is exactly what RPO measures.

Done when:

  • You restored and verified the files.
  • You stated your RPO correctly.

3. See it in real products

Recovery point objectives become product truth. If backups run hourly, a restore can lose up to an hour of work, and the interface and support team must say so honestly. Restore tools also need guarding, because a restore can erase current data.

In security operations

  • Ransomware encrypts file servers, but immutable backups cannot be changed, so the business restores from them without paying.
  • A team discovers its backup job has been failing for months, the first time it attempts a restoration test.

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: A company backs up at midnight every day. A failure occurs at 18:00 and the service is restored at 21:00. How much data was lost at most, and how long was the outage? Which metrics do these correspond to?

Compare your reasoning

Up to 18 hours of data were lost, which relates to RPO. The outage was 3 hours, which relates to RTO. If the business needs an RPO of four hours, daily backups will not meet it.

1. Which metric describes the maximum acceptable data loss?

  • RPO
  • RTO
  • MTBF
  • MTTR
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: RPO. It is measured back in time from the failure.
  • Incorrect: RTO. RTO measures downtime.
  • Incorrect: MTBF. This measures reliability.
  • Incorrect: MTTR. This measures repair time.

2. A device operated for 3,000 hours and failed 6 times. What is its MTBF?

  • 500 hours
  • 6 hours
  • 3,006 hours
  • 18,000 hours
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: 500 hours. 3,000 divided by 6.
  • Incorrect: 6 hours. This is the number of failures.
  • Incorrect: 3,006 hours. The values should be divided, not added.
  • Incorrect: 18,000 hours. The values should be divided, not multiplied.

3. Which backup property best protects against ransomware that deletes backups?

  • Immutability
  • Larger scope
  • Compression
  • Load balancing
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Immutability. Immutable copies cannot be changed or deleted during the lock period.
  • Incorrect: Larger scope. Backing up more does not protect the copies.
  • Incorrect: Compression. Compression saves space.
  • Incorrect: Load balancing. This distributes requests.

4. A team brings the recovery system online alongside production and compares results, without moving users. What is this?

  • Parallel processing
  • Failover
  • Simulation only
  • Capacity planning
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Parallel processing. Both systems run together for comparison.
  • Incorrect: Failover. Failover moves the workload.
  • Incorrect: Simulation only. A simulation does not bring up real systems.
  • Incorrect: Capacity planning. This is about future resources.

5. Match each statement to its metric.

Correct matches
  • Service must return within 2 hours: RTO
  • Lose no more than 15 minutes of data: RPO
  • Repairs take 4 hours on average: MTTR
  • Fails about once every 900 hours: MTBF

Time to restore, data lost, repair duration and failure frequency measure different things.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Swapping RTO and RPO. Time points to downtime, and point refers to a point in the data.
  • Assuming a backup is good because the job reported success.

Summary

  • Back up, keep an immutable copy and test restores.
  • RTO limits downtime, and RPO limits data loss.
  • MTBF measures reliability, and MTTR measures repair speed.
