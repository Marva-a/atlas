Weighing architecture: technical and business considerations

Lesson 31 · Week 8 · Domain 3: Security Architecture · Objective 3.1 · Intermediate · about 120 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Direct product design relevance

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Compare architectures using technical considerations.
  • Compare architectures using business considerations.
  • Make and defend a recommendation.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

There is no single best architecture. Security professionals advise on tradeoffs, and exam scenarios frequently ask which factor is driving a choice.

In plain language

When buying a car, you weigh fuel, seats, price and who will service it. Choosing an architecture is the same, but the factors are availability, recovery, cost and legal requirements.

2. Technical deep dive

Technical considerations: availability is uptime. Resilience is the ability to withstand failure and recover. Proprietary versus open source affects transparency, support and vendor dependence. Usability affects whether people follow secure paths. Responsibility defines who manages what. Compute is the processing capacity needed. Power requirements matter for dense or remote sites. Ease of recovery is how readily a system can be restored.

Business considerations: data sovereignty means data is subject to the laws of the country where it is stored. Data classification decides how strongly it must be protected. Cost includes both up-front capital and ongoing operating expenses. Ownership asks who is accountable for the system and its data. Environmental requirements cover temperature, humidity and physical conditions. Scalability is the ability to grow or shrink with demand. Risk is the overall exposure that the organization is willing to accept.

Walkthrough: Build a decision matrix

  1. List the options side by side.
  2. List the factors that matter, such as availability, cost and sovereignty.
  3. Weight each by importance to this organization.
  4. Score the options and total them.
  5. Write the recommendation and record the residual risk.

3. See it in real products

In multi-tenant SaaS, many customers share one system, and the product's most important promise is that one customer can never reach another's data. Tenant switching, global search, shared caches and support tools are the places where that promise is most likely to break. Data residency turns an architecture decision into a setting and a sales conversation.

In security operations

  • A European health company must keep patient data in the EU, so its cloud regions are chosen to meet that data sovereignty requirement.
  • A tool so awkward that staff bypass it has failed on usability, however strong it is on paper.

4. Apply it as a Product Designer

Design brief: Map tenant isolation and data residency

About 60 min

Scenario: A collaboration product serves companies in Canada, the EU and the US. A large customer asks where its data is stored and what stops another tenant seeing it. Users can also belong to more than one company's workspace.

Problem statement: The product has no clear model of tenant boundaries for users who belong to several tenants, and nothing tells administrators where their data lives.

Your design task:

  • Draw a data flow diagram for a document, from upload to search index, backup and third-party processors.
  • Mark where tenant boundaries and region boundaries are enforced.
  • Design the workspace switcher so a user always knows which tenant they are acting in.
  • Design the data residency panel, including what cannot be moved.

Technical security constraints:

  • Cross-tenant data must never appear in search, autocomplete, notifications or caches.
  • Region promises must include backups and third-party processors.
  • Moving a tenant between regions may be slow, partial or impossible.

User needs:

  • Multi-tenant users need to avoid posting to the wrong company.
  • Customer administrators need accurate facts for their own compliance.
  • Sales needs claims that engineering can stand behind.

Expected deliverable: A data flow diagram with boundaries marked, plus workspace switcher and residency panel designs.

Evaluation criteria:

  • The diagram shows tenant and region boundaries, including backups and processors.
  • Wrong-tenant actions and cross-tenant leaks through search or notifications are addressed.
  • The active tenant is obvious at the moment a user takes an action.
  • Claims in the interface are checked against what engineering can actually guarantee.

Senior-level considerations:

  • Do not write a data promise into an interface until engineering and legal have confirmed it.
  • Note where isolation is a business decision too, because stronger isolation costs more and may be sold as a tier.

A design review is not a security sign-off. Have security engineers confirm technical decisions.

Questions a Senior Product Designer should ask

Engineering:

  • At which layer are tenants isolated: application, database or infrastructure?
  • Which features query across tenants, including internal tools?

Security:

  • How is tenant isolation tested, and how often?

Product:

  • Which customers require a specific data region, and is that a contract term?

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: A Canadian company's contract says customer data must remain in Canada. A SaaS vendor stores data in several regions and cannot restrict it. Which consideration blocks this vendor?

Compare your reasoning

Data sovereignty and location requirements. Unless the vendor can guarantee Canadian storage, the contract cannot be met, whatever the vendor's other strengths.

1. Which term means data is subject to the laws of the country where it is held?

  • Data sovereignty
  • Scalability
  • Compute
  • Usability
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Data sovereignty. The legal jurisdiction follows the location.
  • Incorrect: Scalability. This refers to capacity.
  • Incorrect: Compute. This refers to processing.
  • Incorrect: Usability. This refers to ease of use.

2. Which factor most concerns a remote site that depends on a small generator?

  • Power requirements
  • Open source licensing
  • Data classification
  • Community cloud membership
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Power requirements. Available electricity limits what can run there.
  • Incorrect: Open source licensing. This does not address the power constraint.
  • Incorrect: Data classification. This is about sensitivity.
  • Incorrect: Community cloud membership. This is about shared hosting.

3. Which statement is most accurate?

  • Resilience is the ability to withstand failure and recover, which supports availability
  • Availability and resilience refer only to backups
  • Resilience applies only to on-premises systems
  • Availability means data cannot be read by outsiders
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Resilience is the ability to withstand failure and recover, which supports availability. Resilience is a capability that helps maintain uptime.
  • Incorrect: Availability and resilience refer only to backups. Both are broader.
  • Incorrect: Resilience applies only to on-premises systems. It applies to every architecture.
  • Incorrect: Availability means data cannot be read by outsiders. That is confidentiality.

4. A startup expects rapid, unpredictable growth and has little capital. Which factors favour public cloud? Choose two.

  • Scalability
  • Cost model based on operating expense
  • Physical control of all hardware
  • A requirement for an air-gapped network
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Scalability. Capacity can follow demand.
  • Correct: Cost model based on operating expense. Little up-front spending is needed.
  • Incorrect: Physical control of all hardware. Public cloud does not offer this.
  • Incorrect: A requirement for an air-gapped network. Public cloud cannot be air-gapped.

B. Product design assessment

Rate your work in the interactive app, where it counts toward design competency only.

1. Global search in a multi-tenant app shows matching document titles from every tenant, with content hidden until opened. What should a designer flag?

  • Titles are data, so limit results to the current tenant
  • Results should be grouped alphabetically by tenant name
  • Search should display fewer results on each page
  • Titles are safe because the document body stays hidden
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Titles are data, so limit results to the current tenant. Metadata can be sensitive, and it crosses the most important boundary here.
  • Incorrect: Results should be grouped alphabetically by tenant name. This improves the layout while leaving the leak in place.
  • Incorrect: Search should display fewer results on each page. Smaller pages still reveal other tenants' titles.
  • Incorrect: Titles are safe because the document body stays hidden. Titles can themselves reveal deals, patients or plans.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Choosing an architecture on cost alone.
  • Forgetting that storage location has legal consequences.

Summary

  • Technical factors describe how a design behaves.
  • Business factors decide if it is acceptable.
  • A sound recommendation weighs both and states what risk remains.
