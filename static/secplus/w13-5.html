Audits, assessments and penetration testing

Lesson 55 · Week 13 · Domain 5: Security Program Management and Oversight · Objective 5.5 · Intermediate · about 100 min

Open this lesson in Atlas →

Professional relevance for cross-functional work

1. Learn the concept

By the end, you can

  • Plan an audit, including data gathering and scoping.
  • Distinguish penetration test types and passive and active reconnaissance.
  • Use MITRE ATT&CK, the Cyber Kill Chain and the Diamond Model.

Why it exists and what problem it solves

Audits confirm that controls actually work. Knowing the type of engagement tells you who does what, and with what independence.

In plain language

An audit is a health check by someone looking at your records. A penetration test is hiring a friendly burglar to try the doors. Known environment means the burglar has floor plans. Unknown environment means they have none.

2. Technical deep dive

Data gathering uses sampling, questionnaires/surveys, interviews and assertion, which is a claim to be tested. Reference sources include MITRE ATT&CK, a knowledge base of real attacker techniques; the Cyber Kill Chain, which describes the stages of an intrusion; and the Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis, which relates adversary, capability, infrastructure and victim. Scoping uses an audit charter, which sets authority and purpose, and frequency.

Engagement types include gap analysis; internal work by compliance staff, the audit committee and self-assessments; external work, including examinations, assessments, regulatory reviews and independent third-party audits; and benchmarking against peers or standards.

Penetration testing can be known environment (full details), unknown environment (no details) or partially known environment. It may be physical, offensive (attacking), defensive (defending) or integrated (both sides working together, often called purple teaming). Reconnaissance can be passive, which avoids touching the target, such as public records, or active, which interacts with the target, such as scanning.

Frameworks and standards include industry-based standards such as PCI DSS, international standards such as ISO/IEC 27001, and region-specific standards such as GDPR requirements. Functional testing checks that a control works as designed, and behavioral testing checks how people or systems actually act.

Walkthrough: Scope a penetration test

  1. Obtain written authorization.
  2. Define the systems in and out of scope.
  3. Choose known, partially known or unknown environment.
  4. Agree rules of engagement, timing and emergency contacts.
  5. Report findings, then verify that they were fixed.

3. See it in real products

Audits and penetration tests produce work for product teams and evidence requests that products can make easier. Admin screens that export access reviews and configuration history turn an audit scramble into a routine task.

In security operations

  • A SOC maps its detections to MITRE ATT&CK techniques and finds it has none for credential dumping.
  • Reading a company's job adverts to learn its technologies is passive reconnaissance. Scanning its servers is active.

5. Test your understanding

A. Security+ technical assessment

Exam-style scenario: A company hires testers and gives them no information about its systems. What type of test is this?

Compare your reasoning

An unknown environment penetration test. They begin with nothing, simulating an outside attacker's starting point.

1. Searching public records about a target, without touching its systems, is which?

  • Passive reconnaissance
  • Active reconnaissance
  • A tabletop exercise
  • Fail-open
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Passive reconnaissance. There is no interaction with the target.
  • Incorrect: Active reconnaissance. Active reconnaissance interacts with the target.
  • Incorrect: A tabletop exercise. This is practice for incident response.
  • Incorrect: Fail-open. This is a failure mode.

2. Testers receive complete network diagrams and source code. Which is this?

  • Known environment
  • Unknown environment
  • Partially known environment
  • Passive reconnaissance
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Known environment. Testers have full information.
  • Incorrect: Unknown environment. There testers have no information.
  • Incorrect: Partially known environment. There testers have limited information.
  • Incorrect: Passive reconnaissance. This is one activity, not an engagement type.

3. Match each framework to what it describes.

Correct matches
  • MITRE ATT&CK: Knowledge base of attacker techniques
  • Cyber Kill Chain: Stages of an intrusion
  • Diamond Model: Adversary, capability, infrastructure and victim

They look at an intrusion from different angles.

4. Which offers the most independence?

  • An independent third-party audit
  • A self-assessment
  • A questionnaire filled in by the system's own administrator
  • An informal team conversation
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: An independent third-party audit. It is carried out by an outside, impartial party.
  • Incorrect: A self-assessment. The organization assesses itself.
  • Incorrect: A questionnaire filled in by the system's own administrator. Not independent.
  • Incorrect: An informal team conversation. This is not an audit.

5. Offensive and defensive teams collaborate during a test, and share results as they go. What is this?

  • Integrated testing
  • Unknown environment testing
  • Sampling
  • A legal hold
Answer and explanation
  • Correct: Integrated testing. Both sides work together.
  • Incorrect: Unknown environment testing. This is about knowledge of the target.
  • Incorrect: Sampling. This is a data gathering method.
  • Incorrect: A legal hold. This preserves data.

6. Review and references

Common mistakes

  • Calling scanning passive. Scanning touches the target, so it is active.
  • Starting any test without written authorization.

Summary

  • Audits test claims with evidence.
  • Pen tests differ in knowledge and approach.
  • ATT&CK, Kill Chain and the Diamond Model describe attacks differently.
