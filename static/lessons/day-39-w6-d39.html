Exercise 14: tabletop: a stolen laptop

Day 39 · Week 6 · Advanced · about 120 min

You will run a paper incident for “an employee’s laptop was stolen”, minute by minute: what the admin sees, decides and does in your tailnet and identity provider.

By the end, you can: Walk through a stolen-laptop incident and name the containment, session-revocation and communication steps it requires.

Why it matters to a designer

Tabletops are how security teams rehearse. Running one as a designer shows you the real sequence of screens and decisions under stress, and which are slow or missing. This is a strong interview story about designing for high-stakes moments.

Key ideas

  • A tabletop exercise is an incident practised on paper or in a meeting, with no real harm.
  • Scenario: Friday 18:00, Jordan reports their laptop stolen from a car. It was signed in to the tailnet and the identity provider, with access to production.
  • Minute by minute: who is told, how the admin finds the device, how they remove it from the tailnet, end sessions in the identity provider, rotate any secrets on it, and check logs for use after the theft.
  • Decisions under uncertainty: Was the disk encrypted? Did anyone use the device after 18:00? Should Jordan’s whole account be suspended, or only the device?
  • Afterwards, write a blameless review: what went well, what was slow, and which screen or feature would have helped.
  • Notice the designer’s questions: Can the admin find the device by owner name? Is “remove device” clearly different from “suspend user”? Does the log show activity after a given time?

Key terms

tabletop exerciseA practised incident on paper, used to rehearse a response.
revokeTo cancel access or a credential so it stops working.
session terminationEnding a signed-in session so the user must sign in again.
rotate a secretReplace a key or password with a new one, so the old one stops working.
blameless reviewA review that looks for causes in systems and process, not individual blame.

Check yourself

  • What are the first three things an admin should do when a laptop is stolen?
  • Which screen or decision was slowest in your tabletop, and what would fix it?

What a good answer looks like

  • Typically: remove or disable the device in the tailnet, end the user’s sessions and reset credentials in the identity provider, and check logs for any use after the theft. Then rotate secrets stored on the device.
  • A good answer names a specific moment, such as finding the device by owner or telling device removal apart from user suspension, and a concrete design change with its benefit.
