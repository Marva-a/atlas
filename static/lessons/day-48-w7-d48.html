Case study 1 draft: write it up and share

Day 48 · Week 7 · Advanced · about 120 min

You will write the first full draft of case study 1: problem, research, concepts, design, testing and what changed.

By the end, you can: Write an honest, structured first draft of the access-policy case study.

Why it matters to a designer

This is your first complete portfolio piece in the new domain. A clear, honest story with real evidence is what hiring managers at companies like Tailscale look for, and early feedback from a practitioner makes it much stronger.

Key ideas

  • Structure: the problem and why it matters, your role and constraints, research and evidence, concepts and the choice, the design, testing and changes, outcome and next steps.
  • Lead with the problem and the result in the first screen of the page; busy readers decide in 30 seconds.
  • Show technical understanding plainly: one diagram of how access rules work, with your design on top.
  • Label honestly: this is a self-initiated concept, not shipped work. State who you tested with.
  • Ask one practitioner for feedback with two specific questions, for example “Is the problem real for you?” and “Would you trust this preview?”

Key terms

self-initiatedWork you chose and did yourself, not for a client or employer.
outcomeWhat changed because of the work, or what you expect to change.
feedbackComments from others used to improve your work.

Check yourself

  • What does a reader learn in the first 30 seconds of your case study?
  • How do you label this work honestly?

What a good answer looks like

  • The problem, why it matters, what you did and the result or key insight. A good opening makes the reader want to keep scrolling.
  • As a self-initiated concept project, stating who you researched and tested with and what is assumed. Honesty builds trust with hiring managers.
