Exercise 15: an alert triage view

Day 40 · Week 6 · Advanced · about 120 min

You will design an alert triage view that cuts noise and tells a tired analyst what happened, why it matters and what to do next.

By the end, you can: Design an alert-triage view that separates severity, confidence and impact as distinct fields.

Why it matters to a designer

Triage screens are some of the most used and most criticised screens in security products. A strong design here shows senior skills: information density, prioritisation, and designing for speed under pressure.

Key ideas

  • Start from your Exercise 13 notes: the questions you asked and the context that was missing are your requirements.
  • Triage is sorting: is this real, how bad is it, who should handle it? Your view should answer these in seconds for each alert.
  • Reduce noise by grouping related alerts into one (ten failed sign-ins become one item), by showing confidence, and by letting analysts tune or mute with care.
  • Each alert needs: what happened in plain words, the entities involved (user, device, service) with owners, why it matters (severity and context), and next actions (contain, investigate, dismiss with a reason).
  • Analysts are experts: they want density, keyboard shortcuts and filters, not big friendly cards. Design for experts while keeping the most important thing obvious.

Key terms

severityHow harmful an event could be.
confidenceHow sure the system is that an alert is real.
groupingCombining related alerts into one item.
entityA thing involved in an alert, such as a user, device or service.
information densityHow much useful information fits on a screen without confusion.

Check yourself

  • How does your design reduce noise?
  • What does each alert in your design tell the analyst in the first few seconds?

What a good answer looks like

  • For example by grouping related alerts, showing confidence, hiding low-value alerts behind a filter, and letting analysts tune rules with a recorded reason.
  • What happened in plain words, who or what is involved and who owns it, how severe and how certain it is, and the next action.
