Exercise 11: a local cluster with Calico

Day 31 · Week 5 · Intermediate · about 120 min

You will run a small Kubernetes cluster on your laptop with Calico and deploy a demo app, so pods, services and namespaces become real things you can see.

By the end, you can: Set up a local Kubernetes cluster with Calico and apply a basic network policy.

Why it matters to a designer

Platform engineers live in terminals and YAML. Doing their setup once, slowly and with notes, gives you empathy for their work and a true sense of what is hard, which you will need for case study 2 interviews.

Key ideas

  • kind runs a whole Kubernetes cluster inside Docker on your laptop. It is free and you can delete it at any time.
  • kubectl is the command-line tool for talking to the cluster: kubectl get pods shows what is running.
  • Calico installs as pods in the cluster itself and then controls networking for all other pods.
  • A demo app with three or four small services is enough. Many Kubernetes tutorials have one; any simple web plus backend works.
  • Take notes as a designer: where you waited, what failed, which error messages helped and which did not.

Key terms

kindKubernetes in Docker: a tool for running a cluster on your laptop.
kubectlThe command-line tool for controlling a Kubernetes cluster.
YAMLA text format used to describe Kubernetes objects.
deployTo start an app on a cluster.
CNIContainer network interface: the plug-in that gives pods networking, here Calico.

Install Calico on kind, following the guide step by step. — Installing Calico on kind (Calico Documentation)

25 min

Deploy a small demo app with at least two services in its own namespace. Check it with kubectl get pods and get services.

35 min

Draw your real cluster: namespaces, pods, labels and services, using kubectl output.

25 min

Write designer notes: three moments of confusion and one thing a visual tool could have shown you.

20 min

Skip or skim

Skip cloud clusters, Helm charts and Calico Enterprise features. A local cluster with a small app is the goal.

Check yourself

  • What do kind, kubectl and Calico each do in your setup?
  • What would a platform engineer need from a visual tool that the terminal did not give you?

What a good answer looks like

  • kind runs the cluster in Docker on your laptop, kubectl lets you control and inspect it, and Calico provides pod networking and enforces network policies.
  • For example: a map of which services talk to which, the labels on each pod, and a clear view of what is allowed or blocked, without running many commands.
