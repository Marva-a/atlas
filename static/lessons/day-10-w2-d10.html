Exercise 3: connect two devices with Tailscale

Day 10 · Week 2 · Basics · about 120 min

You will set up a small private network between your laptop and your phone and record, as a designer, every moment of the first run.

By the end, you can: Connect two devices with Tailscale and reach one from the other, and explain why reachability between tailnet devices depends on policy rather than being automatic.

Why it matters to a designer

Onboarding is where networking products win or lose users: if two devices do not connect in the first few minutes, many people give up. Designers at companies like Tailscale study these “it works” moments closely, and you will redesign this flow on Day 12.

Key ideas

  • A tailnet is your own private network: the people and devices you add to it can be reached, and no one outside it can reach in. “Can be reached” is not automatic for every pair, though: an access policy (Week 3) decides which devices on the tailnet may actually reach which others.
  • Sign-in uses an identity provider (Google, Microsoft, GitHub). This is identity-based networking: your account, not your location, decides which network you join.
  • Each device gets a stable address starting with 100. that works wherever the device is: home, a café, a phone network.
  • The “aha” moment is reaching one device from another (for example opening a page served on your laptop from your phone) without touching a router or firewall.
  • Watch for friction: words you did not understand, permissions your phone asked for, waiting with no feedback, and anything that made you unsure whether it worked.

Key terms

tailnetYour private Tailscale network of users and devices.
identity providerThe service that signs you in, such as Google or Microsoft.
clientThe app installed on each device.
coordination serverThe service that shares keys and rules between devices; your traffic does not go through it.
time to valueHow long a new user takes to reach the first useful result.

Check yourself

  • In your own words, what is a tailnet?
  • Why is signing in with Google or Microsoft part of a networking product?
  • What was your “it works” moment, and how long did it take?

What a good answer looks like

  • A private network made only of the people and devices you add. They can reach each other from anywhere, and nothing outside it can.
  • Because access is based on who you are, not where you are. Your identity decides which network you join, and later, rules decide what you may reach.
  • A good answer names the moment (for example, reaching the laptop from the phone) and the time, and notes what nearly stopped you before that.
