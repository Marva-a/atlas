Case study 1: test and improve

Day 47 · Week 7 · Advanced · about 120 min

You will test your policy editor prototype with two people, ideally admins or engineers, and change it based on what you see.

By the end, you can: Test the access-policy-editor prototype with a participant and make one evidence-based improvement.

Why it matters to a designer

Testing turns a nice idea into evidence. Even two sessions with the right people reveal the biggest problems, and a case study that shows “what I learned and changed” is far stronger than one that shows only final screens.

Key ideas

  • Give people a realistic task, not a tour: “Contractors should no longer reach the wiki. Make that change and check nothing else breaks.”
  • Ask them to think aloud. Do not help or explain; note where they hesitate, misread or are surprised.
  • Watch especially for trust: do they believe the effect preview? Would they save the change?
  • After both sessions, sort observations into “must fix”, “should fix” and “later”. Fix the must-fix issues in the prototype today.
  • Record before and after for the case study: the problem you saw, the evidence and the change you made.

Key terms

usability testWatching people try to complete tasks with a design.
think aloudAsking participants to say what they think as they work.
task scenarioA realistic goal given to a participant in a test.
findingAn observation from testing, backed by what you saw.
iterationChanging a design based on what you learned, then testing again.

Check yourself

  • Why give a task rather than a tour in a usability test?
  • What was your most important finding, and what did you change?

What a good answer looks like

  • A task shows whether people can actually do the job with the design. A tour only tells them what you meant, and they will agree politely.
  • A good answer names a real observation (what the person did or said), why it mattered, and the specific change to the design.
