Case study 2 kickoff: why network policies are hard

Day 33 · Week 5 · Intermediate · about 120 min

You will start your second case study by researching why platform engineers find network policies hard, and how Tigera and its competitors try to help.

By the end, you can: Research why network-policy work is hard for platform teams and identify a specific user and problem.

Why it matters to a designer

Case study 2 should show you can design for a highly technical user and a core problem of a company like Tigera. Today’s research grounds it: competitor patterns, public evidence and your own Exercise 12 notes.

Key ideas

  • Your working question: “How might we help platform engineers move from default allow to least-privilege network policy without breaking their apps?”
  • One pattern is policy recommendations: watch real traffic for a while, then suggest policies that allow only what was seen. Calico Cloud does this.
  • Another is staged policies: apply a policy in “would block” mode first, see what it would have blocked, then enforce it.
  • A third is visual traffic maps: Cilium’s Hubble and Calico’s tools show which services talk, so engineers can see before they write.
  • Evidence to collect: forum and GitHub issues, conference talks and blog posts about network policy pain, and one conversation with a platform engineer.

Key terms

policy recommendationA suggested policy generated from observed traffic.
staged policyA policy that reports what it would block without blocking yet.
service mapA picture of which services talk to which.
least privilege (network)Each service may reach only what it truly needs.
platform engineerAn engineer who builds and runs the infrastructure other teams deploy on.
