Polish case study 1: story, outcome, honest labels

Day 57 · Week 9 · Advanced · about 120 min

You will turn case study 1 from a draft into a finished piece: a sharp story, clear visuals, the feedback you received, and an honest outcome.

By the end, you can: Revise the access-policy case study's story and outcome, labelling what was self-initiated, simulated or tested.

Why it matters to a designer

Hiring managers skim. A polished case study shows senior judgement through what you leave out as much as what you include. This is the piece that will start most of your interview conversations.

Key ideas

  • Apply the feedback from Week 7 first; note what you changed and why.
  • Cut to the story: problem, insight, key decisions, result. Move process details to the end or remove them.
  • Make the first screen count: a title that states the problem, one strong image, and a one-paragraph summary.
  • Show impact even without launch: what the tests showed, the metric you would track, and what you would do next.
  • Add the senior layer: the trade-offs you made, how you would work with engineering and product, and how the design would scale to 5,000 devices.
  • Label it as a self-initiated concept and say who you researched and tested with.

Key terms

narrativeThe story structure that connects problem, decisions and result.
scannableEasy to understand by skimming headings, images and captions.
scaleHow a design holds up with many more users, rules or devices.

Check yourself

  • What is the one-sentence summary of case study 1?
  • Which trade-off shows your senior judgement best?

What a good answer looks like

  • A good summary names the user, the problem and the result, for example: “I designed a policy editor that shows admins who will gain or lose access before they save, and in testing both admins trusted the preview enough to save.”
  • A good answer names a real trade-off from your work and the evidence behind the choice, such as plain-language previews versus showing the full rule.
