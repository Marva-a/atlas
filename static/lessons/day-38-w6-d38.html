Exercise 13: find the odd connection in flow logs

Day 38 · Week 6 · Advanced · about 120 min

You will read real flow logs from your cluster or tailnet and find a connection that should not be there, the way an analyst does.

By the end, you can: Investigate a set of flow logs and identify the connection that does not fit the expected pattern.

Why it matters to a designer

Reading data like an analyst moves you from knowing terms to understanding the work. You will see what information is missing, which is exactly where product design adds value: context, grouping and links to policy.

Key ideas

  • This is the first advanced lesson. You will judge real data yourself rather than follow a guide.
  • Plant a problem first: in your cluster, add a small pod that calls the database directly, or in your tailnet, connect from a device that should not reach a server.
  • An analyst asks: Is this normal? Who owns this source? What policy allowed it? Has it happened before? What else did this source talk to?
  • Most raw logs answer only “what” and “when”. The “who owns it” and “should it” answers come from other systems: labels, owners, policies. Joining them is the design opportunity.
  • Write down every question you had and where you found the answer, or whether you could not.

Key terms

baselineWhat normal traffic looks like, used to spot unusual activity.
anomalySomething that differs from the baseline.
contextExtra information that helps judge an event, such as owner or policy.
enrichmentAdding context to raw events automatically.
triageQuickly deciding how urgent an event is and what to do next.

Check yourself

  • What questions does an analyst ask about an unusual connection?
  • What context was missing from the raw flow logs?

What a good answer looks like

  • Is it normal for this source? Who owns it? Which policy allowed it? Has it happened before? What else did this source do? Is the destination sensitive?
  • Usually ownership (which team or person), purpose (what the service is), the policy that allowed or denied it, and history. Raw logs show only addresses, ports, times and verdicts.
