SAML single sign-on and SCIM provisioning

Day 23 · Week 4 · Intermediate · about 120 min

SAML is the older standard companies use for single sign-on into work apps, and SCIM automatically creates, updates and removes accounts in those apps.

By the end, you can: Distinguish SAML-based SSO sign-in from SCIM-based user and group provisioning, and explain why they are not the same operation.

Why it matters to a designer

When a company buys a product like Tailscale or Tigera, one of the first things an admin does is connect it to their identity provider with SAML or OIDC and set up SCIM. These setups are long and error-prone, and they decide whether a deal goes well. You will redesign one on Day 26.

Key ideas

  • SAML does a similar job to OpenID Connect for work apps: the identity provider (Okta, Entra) tells the app “this is Priya, she is in group Design” in a signed message called an assertion.
  • SP-initiated sign-in starts at the app (“Sign in with SSO”). IdP-initiated starts at the identity provider’s dashboard. IdP-initiated is riskier because the app receives an assertion it did not ask for.
  • Setting up SAML means copying values between two admin consoles: URLs, an entity ID and a certificate. One wrong character and sign-in fails with an unhelpful error.
  • SCIM is the joiner–mover–leaver lifecycle, automated. When HR adds someone, the identity provider creates their account in every connected app; when they leave, it deactivates them.
  • SCIM’s core schema defines two resources: users and groups. Deprovisioning usually sets a user to inactive or deletes them.
  • Without SCIM, leavers keep accounts in apps until someone remembers to remove them: the stale accounts from Week 1.

Key terms

SAMLSecurity Assertion Markup Language: an older single sign-on standard for work apps.
assertionThe signed message in which the identity provider tells an app who someone is.
service provider (SP)In SAML, the app the person signs in to.
entity IDA unique name that identifies an app or identity provider in SAML.
SCIMSystem for Cross-domain Identity Management: a standard for creating and removing accounts automatically.
provisioningCreating and updating accounts in apps.
deprovisioningRemoving or deactivating accounts when someone leaves.

What a good answer looks like

  • A signed message from the identity provider to the app, saying who the person is and often which groups they belong to.
  • The app receives an assertion it did not request, so it is harder to be sure it was meant for this sign-in. That makes some attacks easier.
  • When someone is disabled in the identity provider, SCIM deactivates or deletes their account in every connected app automatically, so no stale accounts are left behind.
