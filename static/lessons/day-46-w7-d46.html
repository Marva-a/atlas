Case study 1: concepts, flows and prototype

Day 46 · Week 7 · Advanced · about 120 min

You will design a policy editor that shows admins who will be able to reach what before they save, using everything from Weeks 3 to 7.

By the end, you can: Develop a concept, user flow and prototype for an access-policy editor.

Why it matters to a designer

This is the core design of your first case study. It must show senior skills: several concepts compared, clear reasoning from evidence, and a prototype that handles a complex technical subject in a way admins can trust.

Key ideas

  • Start from your problem statement and research log. Every design choice should link to evidence.
  • Generate at least three concepts before choosing: for example (1) a live “effect preview” beside the editor, (2) a “who can reach X?” query tool, (3) a change summary like the pull request idea from Exercise 16.
  • Use progressive disclosure: show the plain-language effect first (“12 people in Sales will lose access to the wiki”), with the rule details one click away.
  • Design for both worlds: admins who edit in the console and teams who use git. Your preview can appear in both places.
  • Include the hard states: a change that locks everyone out, a conflict with git, a test that fails, and an empty policy for a new tailnet.
  • Build a clickable prototype of the main flow: edit a rule, see the effect, fix a problem, save with confidence.

Key terms

conceptAn early design direction, explored before committing.
effect previewShowing the result of a change before it is saved.
edge caseAn unusual situation the design must still handle.
prototypeA clickable model of a design, used for testing.
design rationaleThe reasons behind design decisions.

Check yourself

  • Why compare three concepts before choosing one?
  • How does your design help an admin trust a change before saving it?

What a good answer looks like

  • It avoids falling in love with the first idea, shows hiring managers your process, and often produces a better solution that combines the strengths of several concepts.
  • For example by showing in plain words who gains or loses access, flagging risky changes, running tests before saving and making rollback easy.
