SSO setup redesign: write it up and share

Day 27 · Week 4 · Intermediate · about 120 min

You will write up Exercise 10 with a short consent-screen audit, showing how you made a technical setup understandable.

By the end, you can: Communicate a redesigned SSO/SCIM experience with a clear before-and-after rationale.

Why it matters to a designer

This piece shows you can work in enterprise identity: long technical flows, two kinds of users (admins and employees) and real constraints. It also prepares you for interview questions about B2B setup and onboarding.

Key ideas

  • Structure: the admin, the current flow and its pain, your redesign, and what you would test.
  • Add a short consent-screen audit: three real consent screens, what each asks for, and how clearly the scopes are explained.
  • Show one technical concept simply, for example the difference between SSO and SCIM in one picture.
  • Be explicit about what you did not know and how you would check it with engineers.
  • Look back at the week: you moved from basic sign-in ideas to enterprise setup. Note which ideas are still shaky.

Key terms

B2BBusiness to business: products sold to companies rather than individuals.
consent auditA review of what permissions apps ask for and how clearly.
assumptionSomething you believe but have not proven yet.

Today's steps

Write the case (500–800 words) with before and after frames.

50 min

Write the consent-screen audit: three screens, what each asks, one improvement each.

25 min

Make one picture that explains SSO versus SCIM simply.

15 min

Share it with one practitioner or post a short version.

15 min

Update your glossary and mark the words you still find hard.

15 min

Skip or skim

Do not include every screen. Choose the moments that show your thinking.

Check yourself

  • Explain SSO and SCIM to a new colleague in two sentences.
  • Which assumption in your redesign would you test first, and how?

What a good answer looks like

  • SSO lets people sign in to an app with their company account. SCIM keeps their account in that app up to date automatically, creating it when they join and removing it when they leave.
  • A good answer picks the riskiest assumption, such as admins wanting a guided wizard, and a simple test, such as watching two admins set it up with a prototype.
