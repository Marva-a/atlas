Case study 1 kickoff: why access policies go wrong

Day 19 · Week 3 · Intermediate · about 120 min

You will start your first portfolio case study by researching a real problem: admins writing access rules that are too open, too tight, or impossible to understand.

By the end, you can: Research a real access-policy failure and frame it as a specific, scoped product opportunity.

Why it matters to a designer

Hiring managers in this domain want to see that you can find a real problem with evidence, not just make screens. This day turns your Exercise 6 experience into research: public evidence, competitor patterns and the first conversations with real admins.

Key ideas

  • A case study starts with a problem, not a solution. Your working question: “How might we help admins change access rules confidently, knowing who will be able to reach what?”
  • Public evidence is everywhere: product docs that warn about mistakes, community forums, GitHub issues and public incident write-ups.
  • Compare how products let admins write and review rules: Tailscale (a policy file and a visual editor), Twingate (resources and groups), Cloudflare (application policies).
  • Talk to real people: 20 minutes with an IT admin or platform engineer is worth hours of reading. Ask about the last time they changed access and what worried them.
  • Keep a research log: every quote, screenshot and link, with where it came from. You will need it for the write-up.

Key terms

case studyA portfolio story of a real problem, your process, your design and its result.
how might weA way to phrase a design problem as an open question.
evidenceFacts that show a problem is real: quotes, data, observations.
competitive analysisComparing how other products solve the same problem.
research logA running list of what you learned and where it came from.

What a good answer looks like

  • For example: “How might we help admins change access rules confidently, knowing who will be able to reach what before they save?” A good question names the user, the task and the outcome.
  • Community forum posts, GitHub issues, product documentation warnings and public incident write-ups. Interviews add private evidence.
