Case study 2 problem statement: write it up and share

Day 34 · Week 5 · Intermediate · about 120 min

You will write the case study 2 problem statement and the journey of a platform engineer securing a cluster.

By the end, you can: Define the user and problem statement for a network-policy product.

Why it matters to a designer

A clear journey shows where the fear and the risk are, which is where your design will act. Sharing it early with engineers keeps your technical understanding honest.

Key ideas

  • The journey: discover what talks to what, write a first policy, test it, roll it out, and keep it right as apps change. Mark the emotions: confident, unsure, afraid.
  • Users: the platform engineer (writes and owns policies), app developers (their apps might break) and the security team (wants least privilege and proof).
  • Evidence comes from your Exercise 12 debugging, public posts and your conversations.
  • Success metrics might be: share of namespaces with policies, time from observed traffic to enforced policy, and incidents caused by policy changes.
  • You have moved from containers basics to a real product problem in one week. Notice that progression in how you write: use the right terms, but explain them.

Key terms

journey mapA picture of the steps a user takes, with their thoughts and feelings.
policy coverageHow much of a cluster is protected by policies.
rolloutPutting a change into effect, often in stages.
stakeholderSomeone affected by or responsible for an outcome.

What a good answer looks like

  • The platform engineer who writes and owns the policies, the app developers whose services could break, and the security team who needs least privilege and proof of it.
  • For example: time from observed traffic to an enforced policy, the share of namespaces protected by policy, or the number of incidents caused by policy changes.
