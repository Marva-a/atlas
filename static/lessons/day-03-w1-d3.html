Exercise 1: be the IT admin in Keycloak

Day 3 · Week 1 · Basics · about 120 min

You will run a real identity provider on your laptop, set up a small company in it, and sign in as one of its employees.

By the end, you can: Create users, groups and roles in a running Keycloak instance, and assign access through group membership rather than per-user edits.

Why it matters to a designer

Admins are one of your main users, and their world is screens full of users, groups, roles and settings. Doing their job once, with your designer’s eye open, teaches you their vocabulary and shows you exactly where admin tools confuse people.

Key ideas

  • Keycloak is a free identity provider, like a small Okta you run yourself. Companies use it to sign people in to their apps.
  • A realm is one company or space in Keycloak. Example: a realm called “acme” for a fictional 40-person company.
  • Users are the people. Groups collect people who share something, like “Design” or “Sales”. Roles are what someone may do, like “viewer” or “admin”.
  • Best practice is to give roles to groups, not to each person. When someone joins Design, they get Design’s access automatically. This is how admins manage hundreds of people.
  • A client is an app that uses Keycloak to sign people in. The getting-started guide includes a small test app (the account console) so you can sign in as an employee.
  • As a designer, watch for three things: words you did not understand, places where you were unsure what a setting would do, and anything you could break by mistake.

Key terms

KeycloakA free, open-source identity provider you can run yourself.
realmIn Keycloak, a separate space for one company or app, with its own users and settings.
userOne person’s account.
groupA set of users who share access, such as a team.
roleA named permission, such as viewer or admin.
clientAn app that relies on the identity provider to sign people in.
DockerA tool that runs software in a ready-made box called a container, so you do not install it piece by piece.

Today's steps

Follow the Keycloak getting-started guide with Docker: start Keycloak, create a realm and a user, and sign in to the account console as that user. — Keycloak: getting started with Docker (Keycloak)

30 min

Set up the company: create 6 users in 3 groups (Design, Sales, IT), create two roles (viewer, admin), and give roles to groups rather than to people.

45 min

Look up anything confusing in the Keycloak admin guide (users, groups and roles chapters only). — Keycloak Server Administration Guide: users, roles and groups (Keycloak)

15 min

Sign in as a Design user and as an IT user. What does each see? Take screenshots.

15 min

Write five designer notes: confusing words, unclear settings and risky buttons, each with a screenshot.

15 min

Skip or skim

Skip themes, identity brokering, federation and client scopes. Users, groups and roles are enough today.

Check yourself

  • What is the difference between a group and a role?
  • Why do admins give roles to groups instead of to each person?
  • Which moment in the admin console would most confuse an IT admin at a small company, and why?

What a good answer looks like

  • A group is a set of people (the Design team). A role is a permission (may view, may administer). You attach roles to groups, so everyone in the group gets those permissions.
  • It scales and stays correct: when someone joins or leaves a team, their access changes with one action, instead of editing many separate settings that are easy to forget.
  • A good answer names a specific screen or word, with a screenshot, says what the admin probably expected, and what happened instead.
